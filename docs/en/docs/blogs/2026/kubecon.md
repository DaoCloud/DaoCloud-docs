# DaoCloud Open Source Advocacy: Eight Years of KubeCon, 112 Talks

In 2018, DaoCloud brought the cloud native practices of Chinese enterprises onto the KubeCon stage; in 2024 and 2025, DaoCloud engineers and the CEO successively served as conference Co-Chairs. Over eight years, what has changed is not just the scale of participation, but the role and influence of DaoCloud in the global open source community.

## What Is KubeCon

KubeCon + CloudNativeCon is the flagship technical conference of the CNCF (Cloud Native Computing Foundation) — the "Kube" in its name is short for Kubernetes. It is the largest and most technically rigorous event in the cloud native space: from Kubernetes itself to scheduling, networking, storage, runtime, and observability, and more recently AI inference and compute scheduling — all under one roof.

The conference is held annually in North America, Europe, China, Japan, and India. In 2026, we traveled to four stops: Amsterdam (Mar 23–26), Yokohama (Jul 28–30), Shanghai (Sep 7–9), and Salt Lake City (Nov 9–12). A stop typically runs 3–4 days and includes keynotes, breakout sessions, hands-on tutorials, lightning talks, maintainer tracks, and project booths. Co-located events often run alongside — CiliumCon, IstioCon, OpenInfra Summit, PyTorch Conference — and the 2026 Shanghai stop was a four-in-one affair.

In terms of scale, KubeCon London 2025 drew over 12,500 attendees, and Amsterdam 2026 approached 13,500 (according to the [official CNCF report](https://www.cncf.io/reports/kubecon-cloudnativecon-europe-2026/)). It is the single most concentrated annual gathering of cloud native and AI infrastructure practitioners worldwide. Who gets to speak at KubeCon, and about what, is to a large extent the technical agenda of the industry itself.

From 2018 to 2021, DaoCloud moved from real-world business scenarios onto the international technical stage. From the cloud native evolution of SPD Bank and the HPC containerization of supercomputing centers to .NET on Kubernetes and Piraeus local persistent volumes, DaoCloud and its customers jointly presented a series of production-grade technical practices. At that stage, DaoCloud focused on telling how cloud native solved real business problems, making the technical practices of Chinese enterprises visible to more developers.

In 2022, DaoCloud stepped from practicing technology into the core of upstream communities. That year, DaoCloud appeared for the first time at the SIG Scheduling maintainer track of KubeCon North America, discussing technical evolution with developers worldwide as a maintainer of Kubernetes core working groups. From using open source technology to solve problems to helping drive the evolution of open source technology itself, DaoCloud completed an important role transition.

In 2023, DaoCloud moved from participating in projects to deep co-building. That year, DaoCloud attended all three KubeCon events worldwide, with open source contributions increasingly concentrated in core technical areas. DaoCloud engineers were active in projects such as Kueue, Karmada, containerd, and KWOK, as well as core Kubernetes working groups including Scheduling, Node, Instrumentation, and Docs. More importantly, DaoCloud no longer only shared technology on stage but also took on the organization of the Kubernetes Contributor Summit (KCS), fostering exchange and collaboration among developers from different countries and regions. From contributing code to connecting communities, DaoCloud began participating in the global open source ecosystem in a deeper way.

In 2024, DaoCloud moved from community co-builder to international conference organizer. That year, DaoCloud proposed advanced technical solutions for challenges such as GPU sharing under AI workloads, large-scale testing, and failure recovery, keeping pace with technological development and frontier innovation while maintaining strong technical influence in the global open source community. An even more symbolic moment happened in Hong Kong: Paco Xu, head of the DaoCloud open source team, served as Co-Chair of KubeCon China, participating in organizing the conference and shaping its technical agenda. In 2025, DaoCloud CEO Roby took over as Co-Chair. From standing on the KubeCon stage to helping build the stage itself, DaoCloud's role in the global open source community moved another step forward.

## Three Transitions Across Eight Years

Breaking down the 112 talks along several dimensions reveals three clear transitions.

**First, from a home stage in China to a majority overseas.**
From 2018 to 2022, there was only 1 overseas talk. From 2023 onward, DaoCloud expanded to both Europe and North America, and in 2025 the first KubeCon Japan arrived: that year 16 overseas talks surpassed the 13 in China for the first time; in 2026 there were 9 overseas and 8 in China, with overseas talks in the majority for the second consecutive year. Over the eight years, overseas talks account for 39% (44/112).

**Second, from cloud native to AI infrastructure.**
Early talks centered on Kubernetes itself: scheduling, storage, networking, runtime. From 2024, the deeply engaged KubeEdge and Karmada, the co-initiated HAMi, and the self-developed Spiderpool became the mainstay; LWS and llmaz appeared in 2025; by 2026 the keywords were already KV-Cache-aware routing, llm-d, P/D disaggregation and TTFT, JobSet, DRA, and LLM inference observability. At the same conference, the language of the talks shifted from "how to manage clusters" to "how to make models run fast".

**Third, from attendee to governor.**
2 Co-Chair terms (2024 Paco Xu, 2025 Roby), 5 keynotes (Hong Kong 2024 ×2, Hong Kong 2025 ×2, Tokyo 2025), and permanent seats such as the Steering Committee, TAG Workloads Foundation Chair, and CNCF Ambassador.

## AI Changed Form Several Times — and Each Time, the Same Four Layers Were Rebuilt

Laying the talks out chronologically reveals another thread more telling than the numbers: each time the shape of AI infrastructure changed, DaoCloud happened to have a corresponding talk on stage:

| Stage | DaoCloud talks that year | What was happening in AI infrastructure |
|---|---|---|
| 2020 | HPC containerization of GPU workloads at a supercomputing center | GPUs started being squeezed into Kubernetes |
| 2023 | Kueue batch scheduling, KubeRay + Kueue managed Ray workloads | Training jobs started queuing, preempting, and quota management |
| 2024 | HAMi heterogeneous GPU, Kueue, KubeEdge | GPUs moved from exclusive use to slicing and sharing |
| 2025 | LWS multi-node inference, llmaz inference platform | Inference moved from single-node feasibility to multi-node collaboration |
| 2026 | KV-Cache-aware routing, llm-d, P/D disaggregation and TTFT, JobSet, DRA, LLM inference observability | Latency, throughput, and cost of inference became the core metrics |

This is not chasing trends. Every time AI changed form, the four layers underneath had to be rebuilt:
**how compute is sliced, how jobs are scheduled, how models are distributed, and how requests are routed**.

What DaoCloud has been doing for eight years is precisely these four layers. So "staying ahead" means, concretely:
**already being in that layer of code before each wave arrived**. How compute is sliced: HAMi was doing GPU slicing when whole-card exclusive allocation was still the norm. How jobs are scheduled: Kueue was building queues, quotas, and preemption before training jobs queued at scale. How models are distributed: MatrixHub accelerates model distribution for vLLM, SGLang, and Dynamo. How requests are routed: DaoCloud was working on P/D disaggregation and KV-Cache-aware routing before llm-d entered CNCF.

## The CFP Only Cares About Technology

KubeCon's CFP only cares about technology: booths can be bought, and top sponsors can buy a limited number of sponsored keynotes or breakout slots, but CFP review ignores sponsor status — getting on stage through the CFP depends solely on how strong the proposal is.

This defines the nature of the number 112: **it is primarily not the output of marketing spend, but of technical judgment being recognized by the community again and again**. Breakout talks must pass CFP review, while maintainer tracks and project lightning talks first require becoming a maintainer of a CNCF project. A company can buy booth space, logos, and receptions with budget, but it cannot buy a maintainer's approval vote for a topic like "topology-aware RDMA allocation in Spiderpool". Over eight years, this has turned into the hardest possible endorsement — if the talks hold up on stage, customers trust you can deliver the work.

Since the bar is technical, what DaoCloud has done over these eight years goes far beyond "attending the conference".

## DaoCloud Advocacy: Sharing Technology and Building Community

Technology diffusion is not one-way preaching but two-way flow — bringing upstream practices back to China while carrying China's production experience into the upstream. KubeCon is just the most visible stretch of that line. Beyond the conference venue:

- **KCD**: organized or co-organized **10 events** in total
  (including Hangzhou in November 2026), with **20+** DaoCloud keynote talks. The 2025 Beijing edition drew about 800 registrations and the Hangzhou edition nearly 300 attendees, making KCD the most consistent in-person cloud native community event in China.
- **4 CNCF Ambassadors**: Roby, Iceber, Paco Xu, windsonsea. An ambassador is not an honorary title; it requires ongoing advocacy, mentoring newcomers, and organizing events.
- **Chinese community**: long-term maintenance of the Chinese localization of the Kubernetes website, with two SIG Docs Chinese Approvers (windsonsea is one of them) and a Kueue Approver among the team. Documentation may look the least "technical", yet it is the first entry point through which most Chinese developers encounter these projects.
- **Public goods inside the venue**: a series of maintainer interview videos produced at KubeCon North America 2023. Such content generates no talk counts, but it is where project teams and users truly talk to each other.

The value of a community never lies in how many talks are given in a year, but in **whether people keep building after the talks end**: contributors met at KCD later submitted PRs, and users met at booths later moved to production.

## Contributing Upstream and Giving Projects Back to the Community

Talks are the result; contributions are the cause. Over these eight years, what deserves even more attention is two things: **contributing upstream, and handing our own projects over to the community**.

Upstream contributions: on the Kubernetes side, there are the Steering Committee (Paco Xu, elected in 2023 and re-elected in 2025 through 2027), kubeadm maintainers, the KWOK founder, Kubespray maintainers, and a group of Reviewers and Approvers across SIG Node / Scheduling / Storage / Docs, with 6 Kubernetes Contributor Awards in total; on the CNCF side, the TAG Workloads Foundation Chair (Paco Xu) and containerd maintainers. Investment in AI has been even heavier in recent years:

- **vLLM**: modules such as the upstream online inference API, request parsing, and Pooling performance contain DaoCloud code.
- **llm-d**: entered the CNCF Sandbox on March 12, 2026 (announced during KubeCon Europe). DaoCloud is listed in the ADOPTERS file as a Contributor (since 2025), focusing on P/D disaggregation and KV-Cache, used in the d.run MaaS platform.
- **Dynamo**: among the 40 external contributors of v1.5.0, DaoCloud ranks first with 8 contributors; since 2026, carlory has moved to Dynamo Operator.
- **HAMi, Kueue, LWS, Karmada, KubeEdge, Spiderpool**: from Reviewer and Approver to maintainer, covering the entire chain of scheduling, GPU slicing, multi-node inference, and multi-cluster.

Handing projects over to the community: HAMi graduated to CNCF Incubating in July 2026; Clusterpedia, HwameiStor, Kubean, Spiderpool, and Piraeus are all in the CNCF Sandbox; KWOK, a kubernetes-sigs project, has been adopted by multiple enterprises and open source projects for large-scale testing and simulation (see [KWOK Adopters](https://kwok.sigs.k8s.io/docs/adopters/)); CloudTTY is listed in the CNCF Landscape. There are also MatrixHub (an enterprise-grade self-hosted AI model registry that accelerates model distribution for vLLM, SGLang, and Dynamo), Unifabric (AI high-speed network observability), KLTS (Kubernetes Long Term Support), kdoctor, egressgateway, miragedebug, and more.

## Conclusion

Broken down year by year and stop by stop, the 112 talks trace a simple path: first take the stage alongside customers, then with the projects DaoCloud maintains, and finally into the seat where the agenda is decided. The number of talks matters less than bringing something new to the stage each time: a new project, a new release, or a solution to a new problem.

Over these eight years, DaoCloud has grown from a practitioner of cloud native technology into a deep contributor to the upstream Kubernetes community and a co-builder of the global open source ecosystem. This is not only DaoCloud's trajectory, but also a microcosm of Chinese developers steadily moving toward the center of the global open source stage.

Looking forward to the next eight years, as AI infrastructure continues to evolve.
